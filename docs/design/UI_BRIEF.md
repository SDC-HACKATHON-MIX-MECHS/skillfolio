# ANTI-SLOP RULES (from the user-supplied 71-minute design-with-Claude video, binding; apply on top of v3/v2)
Lessons the video demonstrates (transcript + frames): (1) written guardrails alone give a generic result; ground each screen in CONCRETE REFERENCES and 'translate, do not copy': keep the composition and restraint, replace everything brand-specific with our own tokens. (2) Work SECTION BY SECTION, one section per pass; rebuilding everything at once becomes a mess. (3) Uniform 2x2 / equal-card grids are the tell of AI-generated layouts: vary card width/height and hierarchy, use a horizontal row or an asymmetric split instead. (4) The 'not slop' result in the video: a calm, limited palette (one deep accent on one neutral), a very large headline with ONE italic serif emphasis word, small mono eyebrow labels, cards that carry an eyebrow + serif title with an italic word, generous space. (5) After each section run a DESIGN-DRIFT CHECK: take a screenshot, compare against the guardrails + references, report concrete deviations, offer fixes, never edit silently. (6) Guardrails drift: when a new pattern appears, add it to the brief.
Concrete anti-slop rules for SkillFolio: no row of 3+ identical cards on any screen (vary the hero card vs the supporting ones); no generic filler copy or stock phrases ('unlock', 'supercharge', 'seamless', 'powerful'); no decorative gradients except the single soft glow behind a page hero; no emoji; no icon-in-circle feature tiles; no fake testimonials/logos/stats; every number and sentence must come from engine data, and role/skill names are shown as-is; one italic serif emphasis word per hero headline at most; align to an 8px grid; labels and body in plain English.
Design-drift check (run before reporting any page done): screenshot at 1280 and 390px, compare against the v3 references (FlowPilot list rows + KPI strip, Vela/Kora hero + eyebrow) and this file; list deviations as a table.

The product must be a web app people actually open: CLEAR, CLEAN, EASY TO READ, with factual numbers. The user finds Trading 212 / Moomoo too cluttered. First screen must answer in 5 seconds: **"Which roles fit me, and why?"** (top 3 roles, fit %, one plain sentence each, one button 'See my learning plan').

Hard rules for every screen:
1. One message per screen: at most 1 hero + 3 cards or rows + 1 chart. At most 4 numbers visible above the fold. Everything else goes behind a 'Show details' expander. No page needs more than ~2 screens of scrolling at 390px.
2. Type: body >= 16px, secondary >= 14px, hero number 48-56px, line-height 1.5. Body text colour at least 7:1 on its background (e.g. #F2F5F9 on #0B0F14); never grey-on-dark smaller than 14px. Fewer borders, more space (>= 24px between sections).

Words banned on the surface: momentum, volatility, covariance, spread, readiness, rebalance, holdings, watchlist, basket, thin data (say 'not enough data').
4. Numbers are factual and sourced: every figure keeps its Source caption (small but >= 13px, in the details area is fine), forecasts say 'estimate', never invent a number. Show fewer numbers, not different ones.
5. One accent colour for the single primary action per screen. Green/red only for up/down with arrow + word. No emoji.
6. Charts: at most one per screen, simple, large labels, hidden behind 'Show trend' where it is not the main message.
7. Tests: add a jargon scan to tests/test_app_smoke.py (AppTest: render each page with a sample profile and assert none of the banned words appear in visible text outside expanders/Methodology) once pages are converted.
